How much does it really cost to run a car in the UK?

Updated 18 October 2026

The monthly payment on the advert is usually less than half of what the car really costs you. Here is a worked example for an ordinary used hatchback, line by line.

The example car

A £16,000 used car bought on PCP with a £2,000 deposit, 9.9% APR over 48 months and a £6,500 balloon at the end. The driver does 8,000 miles a year, gets 45 mpg and pays 138p a litre for fuel.

CostPer monthPer year
Finance payment£240£2,876
Deposit spread over the 4 years£42£500
Fuel (8,000 miles at 45 mpg)£93£1,115
Insurance£75£900
Road tax£16£195
Service and MOT£25£300
Tyres and repairs£21£250
Parking£20£240
Breakdown cover£5£60
Total£536£6,436

So a car advertised at "£240 a month" really costs about £536 a month, or roughly 80p for every mile driven. On a £28,000 salary that is around 27% of take home pay, which is a lot.

Where the money goes

Finance or depreciation

This is normally the biggest single cost. If you pay cash there is no monthly payment, but the car still loses value every year. A car that drops 15% a year costs you about £200 a month in lost value at £16,000, so cash is not free. Our PCP vs HP guide shows how the finance types compare.

Fuel

Fuel cost depends on three things: miles, mpg and the pump price. A UK gallon is 4.546 litres, so at 45 mpg and 138p a litre, each mile costs about 14p in fuel. Drop to 35 mpg and the same 10,000 miles a year goes from about £1,394 to £1,792.

Insurance

For younger drivers and anyone in a city, insurance can be bigger than the finance payment. It is also the cost you can change most easily. See how to cut your car insurance.

Road tax, MOT and servicing

Most cars registered since April 2017 pay a flat standard rate of road tax after the first year, with an extra yearly charge in years 2 to 6 if the list price was over £40,000. Electric cars have paid road tax since April 2025. Check the current figures on GOV.UK. An MOT is needed once a car is three years old and the maximum fee for a car is £54.85. A basic service at an independent garage is usually a few hundred pounds a year.

The small stuff

Tyres, wiper blades, a cracked windscreen, parking permits, tolls and breakdown cover add up. Budgeting £20 to £40 a month for this is sensible for an older car.

Rules of thumb

  • Keep the total cost of the car under about 15% of take home pay. Under 10% is comfortable.
  • Expect the real monthly cost to be roughly double the finance payment.
  • Work out the cost per mile. If it is well over 50p and you do few miles, a cheaper car, car club or train might make more sense. Our cost per mile guide explains how.
