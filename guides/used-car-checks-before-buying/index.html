Used car checks to do before you buy

Updated 18 October 2026

Ten minutes of checks can save you from a car with hidden finance, a clocked mileage or an expensive fault. Most of them are free.

Free checks on GOV.UK

  • MOT history. Shows every MOT result, the mileage at each test and all advisories. Mileage that goes down, or the same advisory year after year, are warning signs.
  • Vehicle information from DVLA. Confirms the make, colour, engine size, tax status and date of first registration match the advert.
  • Vehicle recall check. Shows any safety recalls that may not have been fixed.

The paid check worth doing

A vehicle history check from a provider such as HPI or the AA usually costs between about £10 and £20. It tells you if the car has outstanding finance, has been written off, or is recorded as stolen. If there is finance still owed on it, it can be taken back from you even after you have paid. This is the one check not to skip.

Paperwork

  • The V5C logbook should show the seller's name and the address you are viewing the car at.
  • Check the VIN on the V5C matches the one on the car (usually at the bottom of the windscreen and on a plate in the door frame).
  • Ask for service history and receipts. Stamps with no garage details mean very little.

At the viewing

  • See the car in daylight and start it from cold. Smoke, rattles or warning lights that stay on are reasons to walk away.
  • Check the tyres. The legal minimum tread is 1.6mm, and four new tyres can easily cost several hundred pounds.
  • Test every button: air con, windows, central locking, lights.
  • Take it on a road where you can get above 50 mph if possible.

Then work out the real cost

Once the car passes, get an insurance quote for it and put the price, finance and running costs into the calculator. A cheap car with a high insurance group or poor mpg can cost more each month than a dearer one.
