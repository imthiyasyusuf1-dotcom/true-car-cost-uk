How to work out your car's real cost per mile

Updated 18 October 2026

Most people only think about fuel when they think about cost per mile. The full figure is usually four or five times higher.

Fuel only cost per mile

Use this formula:

Fuel cost per mile (pence) = price per litre (pence) x 4.546 / mpg

At 138p a litre and 45 mpg that is 138 x 4.546 / 45 = about 14p a mile. At 30 mpg it is about 21p a mile.

For an electric car: pence per kWh divided by miles per kWh. Charging at home at 25p per kWh with a car that does 3.5 miles per kWh is about 7p a mile. Public rapid charging can easily be three times that.

Full cost per mile

Add up everything the car costs in a year and divide by the miles you drive:

  • Finance payments, or depreciation if you paid cash
  • Fuel or charging
  • Insurance
  • Road tax
  • Servicing and MOT
  • Tyres and repairs
  • Parking, tolls and breakdown cover

For our example £16,000 car on PCP, that is £6,436 a year over 8,000 miles, or about 80p a mile.

Why it matters

Most of a car's cost is fixed. You pay the finance, insurance and tax whether you drive 2,000 miles or 12,000. So the fewer miles you do, the more each one costs. At 4,000 miles a year the same car would cost well over £1.40 a mile, which is often more than a taxi or train for the trips you actually make.

HMRC mileage rates are not your cost

HMRC's approved rate of 45p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles is a tax free allowance, not a measure of what your car costs. If your full cost per mile is higher than what your employer pays, business driving is costing you money.
