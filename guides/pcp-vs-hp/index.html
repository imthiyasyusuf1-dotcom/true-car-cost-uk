PCP vs HP: which car finance works out cheaper?

Updated 18 October 2026

PCP gives you a lower monthly payment. HP gives you the car. Which is cheaper depends on what you plan to do at the end, so here are the numbers.

Same car, two deals

A £16,000 car, £2,000 deposit, 9.9% APR, 48 months. The PCP deal has a £6,500 balloon payment (the guaranteed future value) at the end.

PCPHP
Monthly payment£240£352
Total interest£4,005£2,875
Paid over 4 years (deposit plus payments)£13,505£18,875
Total if you keep the car£20,005£18,875
Who owns it after the last payment?The lender, unless you pay the balloonYou

If you want to keep the car, HP is about £1,130 cheaper here. That is because on PCP you borrow the balloon amount for the whole four years and pay interest on it the whole time.

When PCP makes sense

  • You want a newer car every three or four years and will hand it back or part exchange it.
  • You are confident you will stay under the mileage limit. Going over usually costs a set amount per mile, often somewhere between 5p and 15p.
  • You will look after the bodywork, because "fair wear and tear" is checked when you hand it back.

When HP makes sense

  • You plan to keep the car for years after the finance ends. The payment then disappears and the car is yours.
  • You do high mileage. HP has no mileage limit.
  • You want to be able to sell or modify the car once it is paid off without asking anyone.

Rights that apply to both

Under the Consumer Credit Act you can usually hand the car back once you have paid half of the total amount payable (this is called voluntary termination). Once you have paid a third, the lender normally needs a court order to take the car back. Read your agreement and check the MoneyHelper car finance guide if you are unsure.

Do not forget the running costs

Finance is only one line. On a typical used car, fuel, insurance, tax and upkeep add another £250 or more a month. See the full monthly breakdown.
